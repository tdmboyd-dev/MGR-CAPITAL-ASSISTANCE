## Industry Overview

### What Are Surplus Funds?

Surplus funds (also called overages, excess proceeds, or excess funds) are the money left over when a property sells at auction for MORE than what was owed in:
- Delinquent property taxes
- Mortgage debt (foreclosure)
- HOA liens
- Other encumbrances

**Example:** Property owes $50,000 in back taxes. Sells at auction for $150,000. The $100,000 difference is the "surplus" that belongs to the former property owner.

### The Business Model

Surplus recovery companies:
1. Research county records to find properties sold at tax/foreclosure auctions
2. Identify former owners entitled to surplus funds
3. Contact owners (often unaware they're owed money)
4. Sign contingency fee agreements (typically 25-40%)
5. File claims with the county/court on behalf of the owner
6. Collect fee when funds are disbursed

### Why Owners Don't Claim Their Own Funds

- **Unaware:** Counties rarely notify former owners they have money
- **Moved:** Address on file is the foreclosed property
- **Deceased:** Heirs don't know about the funds
- **Overwhelmed:** Legal process is intimidating
- **Time constraints:** Working multiple jobs, can't navigate bureaucracy

## Legal Landscape

### Tyler v. Hennepin County (2023)

**Landmark Supreme Court Decision:**
- Ruled 9-0 that taking surplus funds is unconstitutional "equity theft"
- Counties cannot keep excess proceeds from tax sales
- Former owners have constitutional right to surplus
- Expected to generate billions in recoverable claims nationwide

## State-by-State Requirements

### Florida (Most Stringent)

**Surplus Trustee Requirements:**
- Class "A" Private Investigator license required
- Minimum $500,000 liability insurance, cash reserves, OR bonding
- Annual certification through Department of Financial Services
- $25 annual renewal fee
- Application deadline: June 1 each year

**Claim Deadlines:**
- Lienholders: 120 days from Notice of Surplus
- Former owners/heirs: Virtually unlimited (statute of limitations unclear)

**Processing Time:** 4-7 months after claim submission

### Michigan

**Requirements:**
- File Notice of Intent BEFORE auction (by July 1)
- Motion to Claim must be filed within 60 days of notice
- Circuit Court approval required
- **Miss the deadline = forfeit all rights**

### California

**Requirements:**
- Claims must be filed within 1 YEAR of deed recording
- No exceptions to the deadline
- Award challenges must be filed within 90 days
- Funds go to county if unclaimed after 1 year

### Georgia

**Requirements:**
- Minimum 60-day notice period
- Only licensed attorneys can represent claimants (third parties)
- Surplus held in escrow by Tax Commissioner

### North Carolina

**Requirements:**
- File with Clerk of Superior Court
- Verified Motion/Petition required
- Notarized affidavit + Photo ID + W-9
- Assignment agreements scrutinized for fairness
- 30-90 day processing time

### Texas

**Requirements:**
- Property Code Chapter 34 governs surplus
- Claims through District Clerk
- Excess must be claimed within 2 years (constable sales)
- 4 years (sheriff sales)

## Payment Collection Methods

### The Challenge

When the county disburses surplus funds, they send a check to the CLAIMANT (former owner). The recovery company then must:
1. Wait for client to receive check
2. Contact client to arrange payment
3. Hope client actually pays the agreed fee

**Risk:** Client receives funds and ghosts the company ("the windfall problem")

### Solution 1: Assignment of Interest

**How It Works:**
- Client signs "Assignment of Interest" document
- Document assigns a percentage of the claim to the company
- Filed with the court as part of the claim
- Court order directs county to issue TWO checks (client + company)

**States Where This Works:** Florida, Texas, Georgia, California (some counties)

**Limitations:** Some courts/counties refuse to honor assignments

### Solution 2: Attorney Trust Account (IOLTA)

**How It Works:**
- Attorney signs as representative
- Court directs funds to attorney trust account
- Attorney deducts fee and disburses remainder to client

**Requirements:** Must use a licensed attorney in the state

### Solution 3: Escrow Service

**How It Works:**
- Use third-party escrow (Escrow.com, similar)
- County sends funds to escrow
- Escrow auto-splits per agreement
- Both parties receive their share

**Costs:** Escrow fees (typically 1-3%)

### Solution 4: Direct County Split Payment

**How It Works:** Some counties will issue split checks if:
- Power of Attorney authorizes it
- Assignment is notarized and filed
- Court order specifically directs it

**Availability:** County-dependent, must verify

## SECTION 1: STATE-BY-STATE SURPLUS RECOVERY RULES (Detailed 2025-2026)

### Constitutional Foundation: Tyler v. Hennepin County (2023)

The U.S. Supreme Court unanimously ruled (9-0) that it is an unconstitutional taking under the Fifth Amendment for a municipality to retain excess funds from a tax lien foreclosure sale. This landmark decision has triggered a nationwide wave of legislative reforms. States must now implement mechanisms for property owners to claim surplus proceeds, though the Court did not prescribe a specific remedy, leaving states to craft compliant procedures.

**Impact Summary:**
- States that previously kept surplus (MA, OR, NJ, CO, AZ, MN, NE) are NOW required to return excess funds
- Created billions in newly recoverable claims nationwide
- Private lienholders can be considered "state actors" liable for takings (NJ Supreme Court ruling, January 2025)
- Retroactivity varies by state -- some only apply to sales after May 25, 2023

### TEXAS (High Volume -- Priority State)

**Governing Law:** Texas Tax Code Chapter 34, Sections 34.03 and 34.04
**Recent Amendment:** SB 766, 89th Legislature (Acts 2025, Ch. 986), effective September 1, 2025

**Claim Process:**
1. File a petition in the court that ordered the seizure or sale
2. Petition may be filed under the cause number of the underlying suit (no separate suit required)
3. Copy of petition must be served on all parties not later than 20th day before hearing date
4. Court orders payment at hearing based on priority

**Filing Deadline:** 2 years from the date of sale (after which funds forfeit to taxing units)

**Required Documents:**
- Petition filed with district court
- Proof of ownership/interest at time of judgment
- Service on all parties (Rule 21a, Texas Rules of Civil Procedure)

**Notarization Requirements:**
- Assignment documents must contain a sworn statement (effectively notarized)
- Assignor signature required on written instrument

**Third-Party / Recovery Company Rules:**
- **Attorney fee cap:** 25% of amount recovered OR $1,000, whichever is LOWER
- **Non-attorneys PROHIBITED from charging fees** to recover excess proceeds for an owner
- **Assignment requirements (strict):**
  - Cannot be taken until 36th day after excess proceeds deposited in court registry
  - Must be in writing, signed by assignor
  - Must NOT result from in-person or telephone solicitation
  - Assignee must pay at least 80% of the claim amount at time of assignment
  - Court-ordered payment capped at 125% of what assignee paid
  - Must include sworn statement with 8+ required disclosures
  - Assignee must produce original assignment document in court
  - Non-compliance = liability for full amount obtained + attorney's fees

**Notification:** Clerk must send written notice to former owner within 30 days if excess exceeds $25

**Bot Intelligence Notes:**
- Texas is extremely strict on third-party recovery -- attorney involvement mandatory
- The 80% minimum consideration rule means recovery companies must front significant capital
- Solicitation restrictions prohibit cold calling/door knocking for assignments
- Two-year window is relatively generous but still requires urgency
- Each of Texas's 254 counties may have procedural variations

### FLORIDA (Highest Volume -- Most Competitive)

**Governing Law:** Florida Statute Section 197.582

**Claim Process:**
1. Clerk issues "Notice of Surplus Funds From Tax Deed Sale" to all potential claimants
2. Claimants submit notarized claim to Clerk and Comptroller
3. Claims can be submitted by mail, email, fax, or hand delivery
4. After claim window closes, County Attorney determines distribution per priority in F.S. 197.582
5. Determination takes 4-7 months

**Filing Deadlines:**
- **Lienholders (non-governmental):** 120 days from Notice date -- HARD DEADLINE, no exceptions
- **Governmental lienholders:** 120 days from Notice date
- **Property owners/heirs:** Extended -- up to 1 year with Clerk, then INDEFINITELY via State unclaimed property (Florida Statute 717.117)
- **Federal lienholders:** NOT barred by 120-day rule

**Required Documents:**
- Notarized claim form (county-specific)
- State-issued photo ID, business ID, or equivalent
- Proof of entitlement: recorded deed, lien, mortgage, probate records
- For heirs: probate records may be required
- In Broward County: original documents with wet signatures ONLY (no email submissions)

**Notarization Requirements:**
- MANDATORY -- all claims must be notarized
- Broward County requires original notarized affidavit
- Missing or improper notarization = months of delay or permanent loss

**Third-Party / Recovery Company Rules:**
- **Fee cap: 12%** of surplus (F.S. 45.033(3)(d))
- WARNING: Companies often add "consultation fees" of up to 60% on top of 12%
- Claimants must sign over ALL rights to the recovery company
- Third-party companies CANNOT collect surplus funds directly on behalf of clients
- **PI License (Class A) required** to operate as surplus trustee
- $500,000 liability insurance, cash reserves, OR bonding required
- Annual certification through Department of Financial Services

**After 1 Year (Unclaimed):**
- Funds transfer to State of Florida
- Can be claimed indefinitely through Florida's Treasure Hunt website
- No statute of limitations on unclaimed property

**Liens That SURVIVE Florida Tax Deed Sales:**
- Government liens survive
- Liens that do NOT survive: personal loans, credit card judgments, HOA liens, child support liens, mechanics liens, mortgage liens

**Bot Intelligence Notes:**

**State Guidance:** California State Controller's Excess Proceeds Guide 2025

**Claim Process:**
1. Any party of interest may file a written claim with the county
2. Claims must contain sufficient information and proof to establish right to proceeds
3. Separate claim form required for each owner of record (including spouses)
4. Board of supervisors establishes information/proof requirements (varies by county)

**Priority of Claimants:**
1. First: Lienholders of record (in order of priority) prior to deed recording

**Required Documents:**

**County-Specific Examples:**
