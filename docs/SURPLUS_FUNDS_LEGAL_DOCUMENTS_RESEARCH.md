## 1. REQUIRED LEGAL DOCUMENTS

### 1A. Client Service Agreement / Contingency Fee Agreement

**This is the foundational document in surplus recovery.** The industry standard is a **contingency fee agreement** (not a traditional retainer). The finder/recovery agent receives a percentage commission only upon successful recovery, with no upfront payment from the client.

**Must Include:**
- Full legal names of all parties (Client/Claimant and Agent/Recovery Company)
- Description of the surplus funds (property address, sale date, county, case number)
- Estimated surplus amount (if known)
- Fee percentage (industry standard: 10%--40% depending on state caps)
- Scope of services (research, filing, court appearances, follow-up)
- Duration / termination clause
- Expense responsibility (who pays filing fees, title searches, etc.)
- Confidentiality clause
- Dispute resolution (arbitration vs. litigation)
- Governing law / jurisdiction
- E-signature / wet signature block with date
- Notarization block (required in many jurisdictions)
- Statement that no upfront payment is required
- Cancellation/cooling-off period (3--5 business days recommended)

---

### 1B. Limited Power of Attorney (POA)

**Purpose:** Authorizes the recovery agent to act on behalf of the claimant to file claims, communicate with courts/clerks, and receive information (but NOT funds directly in most cases).

**Must Include:**
- Principal's full legal name, address, date of birth
- Agent's full legal name, company name, address
- Specific powers granted (narrowly scoped):
  - Filing claims with county/court clerk
  - Requesting information about surplus amounts
  - Communicating with government offices
  - Signing claim forms on behalf of principal
  - Receiving correspondence related to the claim
- Powers explicitly NOT granted (receiving funds directly, settling for less without consent)
- Effective date and expiration date
- Revocation clause
- Notarization block (REQUIRED in virtually all jurisdictions)
- Witness signatures (two witnesses recommended)

**CRITICAL NOTE:** Some counties (e.g., Chatham County, GA; Hall County, GA) do NOT accept POAs from non-attorneys. Georgia requires a licensed attorney with a state bar number. Always verify county acceptance before relying on a POA.

---

### 1C. Assignment of Rights / Assignment of Proceeds

**Purpose:** Alternative to POA -- transfers the client's right to claim surplus funds to the recovery company. More powerful than POA but requires higher client trust.

**Two Types:**
1. **Revocable Assignment** -- Client can cancel at any time
2. **Irrevocable Assignment** -- Cannot be revoked without mutual consent

**Must Include:**
- Assignor (client) and Assignee (recovery company) full legal details
- Property description and case/sale reference numbers
- Specific rights being assigned (right to file claim, receive funds, etc.)
- Consideration (the fee arrangement)
- Whether partial or full assignment
- Duration and termination conditions
- Governing law
- Notarization (strongly recommended / often required)
- Witness signatures

**Example Template Source:** [Stanislaus County Assignment of Rights to Collect Excess Proceeds](https://www.stancounty.com/tr-tax/pdf/assignment-form.pdf)

---

### 1D. Affidavits (Multiple Types)

#### 1D-i. Affidavit of Heirship
**Used when:** The original property owner is deceased and heirs are claiming surplus funds.

**Must Include:**
- Decedent's full legal name, date of birth, date of death, last known address
- Decedent's marital status at time of death
- Names and relationships of ALL surviving heirs
- Statement of whether a will exists
- Statement of whether probate has been opened
- Description of the property and surplus funds at issue
- Affiant's relationship to the decedent
- Statement that affiant has personal knowledge of the family relationships
- Jurat (sworn oath) and notarization
- Two disinterested witnesses (persons with no financial interest)

#### 1D-ii. Small Estate Affidavit
**Used when:** The estate value falls below the state's small estate threshold and full probate is not required.

**Must Include:**
- Decedent's information
- Statement that the estate value is below the statutory threshold
- List of all estate assets
- List of all known creditors
- Heirs and their shares under intestacy law
- Notarization

#### 1D-iii. Heirship / Pedigree Affidavit
**Used in:** All estate proceedings to identify distributees (heirs).

**Must Include:**
- Full family tree of the decedent
- Identification of spouse, children, parents, siblings, nieces/nephews, grandparents, aunts/uncles, cousins
- For distributees at grandparent level or beyond: affiant must have no financial interest
- Notarization

#### 1D-iv. Affidavit of Identity
**Used to:** Verify the claimant is who they say they are.

**Must Include:**
- Full legal name, date of birth, SSN (last 4), current address
- Statement of identity under oath
- Attached copy of government-issued photo ID
- Notarization

#### 1D-v. Affidavit of Entitlement / Claim Affidavit
**Used to:** Establish legal right to the surplus funds (ownership at time of sale).

**Must Include:**
- Claimant's full legal name and contact information
- Property address and legal description
- Foreclosure/tax sale case number and date
- Claimant's relationship to the property (owner, lienholder, heir)
- Statement of entitlement under oath
- Supporting documentation references
- Notarization

---

### 1E. Motions to the Court

#### 1E-i. Motion/Petition for Disbursement of Surplus Proceeds
**The core court filing.** Required in most jurisdictions to release surplus funds.

**Must Include:**
- Case caption (court name, case number, parties)
- Statement of facts (property description, sale date, sale price, surplus amount)
- Legal basis for the claim (state statute citation)
- Claimant's entitlement (ownership, heirship, lien priority)
- List of all known interested parties
- Prayer for relief (specific dollar amount requested)
- Verification (sworn statement that contents are true)
- Certificate of service (proof all parties were notified)
- Proposed Order for the judge to sign

#### 1E-ii. Proposed Order of Disbursement
**Accompanies the motion.** Pre-drafted order for the court to sign.

**Must Include:**
- Case caption
- Findings of fact
- Conclusions of law
- Order directing the clerk to disburse specific amount to specific payee
- Payee's full legal name and address for check issuance
- Judge signature block

---

### 1F. Cover Letters to Clerks

**Purpose:** Professional cover letter accompanying the filing packet, making it easy for the clerk to process.

**Must Include:**
- Date and clerk's office address
- Re: line with case number, property address, and "Claim for Surplus Funds"
- Brief summary of what is enclosed
- Checklist of enclosed documents
- Return address and contact information
- Filing fee (check or money order) reference
- Request for confirmation of receipt
- Professional letterhead

---

### 1G. Filing / Claim Packets

**Complete filing packet typically contains:**

1. Cover letter to clerk
2. Motion/Petition for Disbursement (original + copies per local rules)
3. Verified Affidavit of Entitlement
4. Special Proceeding Summons (e.g., AOC-SP-100 in NC)
5. SCRA Declaration (AOC-G-250 in NC) for non-appearing respondents
6. W-9 for each payee
7. Notarized Assignment/Engagement Letter (if using a recovery company)
8. Proposed Order of Disbursement
9. Filing fee (typically $45--$120 depending on jurisdiction)
10. Certificate of Service

---

### 1H. Evidence Packets

**Supporting documentation organized as exhibits:**

- **Exhibit A:** Property deed / recorded deed showing ownership at time of sale
- **Exhibit B:** Foreclosure judgment / tax sale certificate / trustee's final report
- **Exhibit C:** Title search / lien search results
- **Exhibit D:** Death certificate (if owner is deceased)
- **Exhibit E:** Probate documents (Letters Testamentary / Letters of Administration)
- **Exhibit F:** Affidavit of Heirship (if applicable)
- **Exhibit G:** Government-issued photo ID of claimant
- **Exhibit H:** Power of Attorney or Assignment of Rights (if applicable)
- **Exhibit I:** Mortgage statements / payoff letters showing liens satisfied
- **Exhibit J:** Chain of title documentation

---

### 1I. Verification Letters

**Types:**
1. **Surplus Verification Letter** -- Request to clerk to confirm surplus amount on deposit
2. **Identity Verification Letter** -- Confirms claimant's identity through third-party verification
3. **Attorney Verification Letter** -- Attorney certifies they represent the claimant (includes bar number)
4. **Agent's Certification of Authority** -- Certifies POA is in effect and grants specific authority

---

### 1J. Payment Instruction Forms

**Must Include:**
- Payee's full legal name (must match court order exactly)
- Payee's mailing address for check delivery
- W-9 (Request for Taxpayer Identification Number and Certification)
- Wire transfer instructions (if applicable -- bank name, routing number, account number)
- Direct deposit authorization (if offered by jurisdiction)
- Signed authorization for payment method

---

### 1K. Client ID Verification Forms

**Required Documents from Client:**
1. Government-issued photo ID (driver's license, passport, state ID)
2. Social Security card or ITIN documentation
3. Proof of current address (utility bill, bank statement within 60 days)
4. IRS Form W-9 (signed and completed)
5. Birth certificate (for heir claims)
6. Marriage certificate (for spousal claims)
7. Legal name change documentation (if names don't match records)

---

### 1L. Probate-Specific Documents (When Owner is Deceased)

1. **Certified Death Certificate** -- Must be certified copy, not photocopy
2. **Letters Testamentary** -- If there is a will and executor appointed
3. **Letters of Administration** -- If no will (intestate) and administrator appointed
4. **Last Will and Testament** -- Certified copy from probate court
5. **Order Admitting Will to Probate** -- Court order accepting the will
6. **Affidavit of Heirship** -- Identifies all legal heirs
7. **Small Estate Affidavit** -- For estates below statutory threshold
8. **Heirship/Pedigree Affidavit** -- Family tree documentation
9. **Birth Certificates** -- Proving parent-child or sibling relationships
10. **Marriage Certificates** -- Proving spousal relationship
11. **Divorce Decrees** -- If applicable to establish current marital status
12. **Creditor Notice Publication** -- Must publish notice and wait 3+ months before final disbursement

---

### 1M. SCRA (Servicemembers Civil Relief Act) Declaration

**Required when:** Any respondent/interested party has not appeared in the proceeding.

**Purpose:** Certifies that the non-appearing party is not an active-duty servicemember entitled to SCRA protections.

**Must Include:**
- Statement of due diligence in determining military status
- Results of SCRA database search (Defense Manpower Data Center)
- Date of search
- Non-appearing party's name and identifying information
- Standard form (e.g., AOC-G-250 in NC)

## 2. BEST PRACTICES FOR EACH DOCUMENT

### 2A. Legal Language Requirements

- Use **plain English** where possible, but include necessary legal terms of art
- Include **definitions section** at the beginning of all agreements
- Use **specific statutory references** (e.g., "pursuant to O.C.G.A. 48-4-5" or "under Florida Statute 45.033")
- Include **severability clause** (if one provision is invalid, the rest survives)
- Include **merger/integration clause** (this document is the entire agreement)
- Use **"WHEREAS" recitals** to establish context in formal legal documents
- All court filings must use **verification language** ("Under penalty of perjury, I declare...")

### 2B. Notarization Requirements

| Document | Notarization Required? |
|---|---|
| Contingency Fee Agreement | Recommended (required in some states) |
| Limited Power of Attorney | YES -- Required in virtually all states |
| Assignment of Rights | YES -- Strongly recommended / often required |
| Affidavit of Heirship | YES -- Always required |
| Small Estate Affidavit | YES -- Always required |
| Affidavit of Identity | YES -- Always required |
| Motion/Petition (verified) | YES -- Verification requires notarization |
| W-9 | NO -- Signature only |
| Cover Letter | NO |
| Proposed Order | NO |

### 2C. E-Signature Compliance (ESIGN Act & UETA)

**Federal Foundation:**
- The **ESIGN Act** (2000) provides federal baseline: electronic signatures have the same legal validity as wet ink signatures
- **UETA** (adopted by 49 states + DC) provides state-level rules
- Both require **explicit consent** from all parties to use electronic signatures

**Requirements for Valid E-Signatures:**
1. **Intent to sign** -- The signer must intend to sign the document
2. **Consent to do business electronically** -- Must be obtained before presenting documents
3. **Association** -- The e-signature must be logically associated with the record
4. **Record retention** -- Electronic records must be accurately preserved and accessible
5. **Opt-out right** -- Consumers must be able to withdraw consent and receive paper copies

**Exceptions (E-Signatures NOT valid for):**
- Wills, codicils, testamentary trusts
- Certain UCC provisions
- Court orders and notices
- Cancellation of health/life insurance
- Hazardous materials transport documents

**For Surplus Recovery Specifically:**
- Client Service Agreements: E-signature VALID in most states
- POA: E-signature may be valid but **notarization still required** -- use RON
- Affidavits: E-signature valid ONLY if notarized via RON in RON-approved states
- Court filings: Must comply with court's specific e-filing rules
- W-9: IRS accepts electronic signatures

### 2D. Formatting Best Practices

- Use **12-point font**, Times New Roman or similar professional font
- **Double-spaced** for court filings; single-spaced for agreements
- **Numbered paragraphs** for all legal documents
- **Page numbers** on every page
- **Initials line** at the bottom of each page for multi-page agreements
- **Header/footer** with case number on court filings
- **Professional letterhead** for cover letters and correspondence
- **Exhibit labels** clearly marked (Exhibit A, B, C, etc.)

## 4. COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS

### 4A. Federal Compliance

1. **Tyler v. Hennepin County (2023)** -- U.S. Supreme Court unanimously ruled it is unconstitutional for government to keep surplus from tax sales. This is the foundational case.

2. **ESIGN Act Compliance** -- All electronic agreements must meet federal e-signature standards (consent, intent, retention).

3. **SCRA Compliance** -- Must verify military status of all non-appearing parties before court enters judgment.

4. **IRS Reporting** -- W-9 required for all payees; surplus funds may be taxable income.

5. **Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / KYC** -- Recovery companies handling funds should implement basic KYC procedures:
   - Government-issued photo ID verification
   - Address verification
   - W-9 / TIN verification
   - Screening against OFAC sanctions lists

### 4B. State-Level Compliance

**Licensing Requirements:**
- **Texas:** Non-attorneys cannot charge for surplus recovery services
- **Georgia:** Some counties require a licensed attorney with bar number
- **Florida:** Surplus trustee must locate owner within 1 year of appointment
- **North Carolina:** NC-licensed attorney must file; out-of-state partners cannot file
- **California:** Claim deadline is 1 year from tax deed recording date

**Contract Requirements:**
- Agreements must be **in writing**
- Must specify the **exact fee percentage**
- Must be executed **after** the tax sale (Florida)
- Must include **cancellation/cooling-off provisions**
- Subject to examination for **fairness, capacity, and notarization**

**Prohibited Practices:**
- Charging fees during certain holding periods (Colorado)
- Having clients sign away rights before the sale occurs
- Misrepresenting the complexity of the claim process
- Adding hidden fees on top of disclosed contingency percentage
- Endorsing checks, changing mailing addresses, or settling without clear written authorization

### 4C. Ethical Compliance

- **No misleading solicitation letters** that look like government notices
- **Clear disclosure** that clients can file claims themselves without a recovery agent
- **Reasonable fees** that do not exploit vulnerable populations
- **No coercion** or high-pressure sales tactics
- **Privacy protection** for all client personal and financial information

## 5. DOCUMENT SIGNING PROCESS

### 5A. How Top Companies Handle Signing

**Three-Tier Approach Used by Industry Leaders:**

#### Tier 1: E-Signature (for non-notarized documents)
- **Platform:** DocuSign, HelloSign, PandaDoc, or similar
- **Documents:** Client Service Agreement, W-9, Payment Instructions, Cover Letters
- **Process:**
  1. Agent prepares documents using templates
  2. Documents sent to client via e-signature platform
  3. Client reviews and signs electronically
  4. Platform captures audit trail (IP address, timestamp, device info)
  5. Signed documents stored in secure cloud with tamper-evident seals

#### Tier 2: Remote Online Notarization (RON) (for notarized documents)
- **Platform:** DocuSign Notary, Notarize.com, NotaryCam, Nexsys CLOSEmore
- **Documents:** POA, Assignment of Rights, Affidavits, Verified Petitions
- **Process:**
  1. Documents uploaded to RON platform
  2. Client receives secure link via email
  3. Client completes tech check (camera, mic, internet)
  4. Identity verification: government-issued ID photo + knowledge-based authentication (KBA)
  5. Live audio-video session with commissioned notary
  6. Client signs electronically while notary observes
  7. Notary applies electronic seal and signature
  8. Session recorded and stored (required retention period varies by state)
  9. Tamper-evident Certificate of Completion generated

**RON is now legally accepted in 40+ states.** Early adopters include Virginia, Florida, and Texas. Adoption accelerated post-COVID.

#### Tier 3: Traditional Wet Signature + In-Person Notarization
- **Used when:** RON not available in jurisdiction, client preference, or court requirement
- **Documents:** Any document requiring notarization in non-RON states
- **Process:**
  1. Documents mailed or emailed (for print) to client
  2. Client visits local notary (UPS Store, bank, mobile notary)
  3. Documents signed in ink, notarized with physical seal
  4. Originals mailed back via tracked delivery (FedEx/UPS)
  5. Agent receives, scans, and files originals with court

### 5B. Recommended Signing Workflow for Maximum Efficiency

```
STEP 1: Client Intake
  - Verify client identity (photo ID + address verification)
  - Confirm eligibility (property ownership, heir status)
  - Run preliminary surplus amount check

STEP 2: E-Sign Non-Notarized Documents (Same Day)
  - Client Service Agreement (contingency fee)
  - W-9 (IRS form)
  - Payment Instruction Form
  - Client Information Sheet

STEP 3: RON Session (Schedule within 48 hours)
  - Limited Power of Attorney
  - Assignment of Rights (if using instead of POA)
  - Affidavit of Entitlement
  - Affidavit of Heirship (if deceased owner)

STEP 4: Attorney Prepares Court Filings
  - Motion/Petition for Disbursement
  - Proposed Order
  - Certificate of Service
  - SCRA Declaration

STEP 5: File with Court
  - Submit complete filing packet
  - Pay filing fees
  - Serve all interested parties

STEP 6: Follow Up
  - Track case progress
  - Respond to court requests
  - Attend hearings if required
  - Receive and distribute funds
```

## 6. STATE-SPECIFIC VARIATIONS

### Georgia
- **Statute:** O.C.G.A. 48-4-5
- **Deadline:** 5 years from tax sale date; then funds transfer to GA Department of Revenue
- **Finder Fee Cap:** 5%
- **Attorney Required:** Yes (some counties); Chatham County and Hall County do NOT accept POAs from non-attorneys
- **Process:** File interpleader action in Superior Court
- **Key Form:** County-specific claim forms (must be unaltered)

### Florida
- **Statute:** Florida Statute 45.033
- **Deadline:** 60 days from sale for claims; surplus trustee has 1 year to locate owner
- **Finder Fee Cap:** 12% statutory cap on surplus recovery companies
- **Process:** Petition to court; surplus trustee files petition on behalf of owner
- **Key Form:** Notarized statement of claim
- **Special:** Judicial foreclosure state -- court oversees the entire process

### Texas
- **Deadline:** Varies by county
- **Finder Fee Cap:** Lesser of 25% or $1,000
- **Attorney Required:** Yes -- non-attorneys cannot charge for surplus recovery
- **Process:** File verified claim with county auditor; if approved, county auditor issues warrant
- **Key Form:** Verified claim form

### California
- **Deadline:** 1 year from tax deed recording; 3 years from foreclosure sale; then escheats to state
- **Finder Fee Cap:** No specific cap
- **Process:** File formal claim with county; motion for disbursement of surplus
- **Key Form:** Motion for Disbursement (Sacramento County template available)
- **Template:** [Sacramento County SBS Motion Template](https://saclaw.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/SBS-Surplus-Funds-Motion-Template.rtf)

### New York
- **Statute:** RPT Article 11, Title 6, Section 1197
- **Deadline:** 3 years minimum for residential property from confirmation of sale report
- **Finder Fee Cap:** No specific cap; subject to court approval
- **Filing Fee:** $45 e-filing fee
- **Process:** File motion with court; Court-appointed Referee collects funds
- **Key Forms:** [NY Courts Surplus Monies Forms](https://www.nycourts.gov/forms/foreclosure/index.shtml)
- **Self-Filing:** Step-by-step instructions available from NY Courts

### North Carolina
- **Process:** File verified petition in foreclosure county Clerk of Superior Court
- **Attorney Required:** NC-licensed attorney must file
- **Service:** Must serve all junior lienholders via special proceeding summons (Rule 4)
- **Key Forms:** AOC-SP-100 (summons), AOC-G-250 (SCRA declaration)
- **Filing Costs:** ~$120 + title search ($150--$250)
- **Fee Range:** 25%--40% contingency (attorney fees)

### Colorado
- **Finder Fee Cap:** 20% after holding periods expire
- **Special:** Contracts void during public trustee custody AND first 2 years of state treasurer custody
